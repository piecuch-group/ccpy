Analytic gradients and finite differences

An energy gradient is the derivative of a specified scalar energy surface. Before using a derivative, choose the electronic method, frozen core, correction variant and (for excited states) returned root. The same choices must be used at every displaced or optimized geometry. Geometry optimizers need the gradient of the energy they are minimizing, including any enabled energy completion.

For relaxed multipole moments and vibrational IR intensities, see Multipole moments and harmonic IR intensities. Property-perturbation requests have their own return shapes and analytic availability; their fallback differs from nuclear gradients.

The common method interface

from ccpy import Molecule, RHF, CCSD

mol = Molecule("O 0 .05 .1173; H 0 .7572 -.4692; H .1 -.7572 -.4692",
               "6-31g", auxiliary_basis="def2-universal-jkfit",
               symmetry=False)
cc = CCSD(RHF(mol, e_tol=1.e-12, d_tol=1.e-10), nfrozen=1,
          amp_convergence=1.e-10, energy_convergence=1.e-12,
          fd_step=1.e-3, fd_npoints=4)
print(cc.has_analytic_gradient())
energy, gradient = cc.energy_and_gradient()
assert cc.converged
print(energy, gradient)  # Eh; array (natoms, 3), Eh/bohr

gradient() automatically chooses the analytic route when has_analytic_gradient() permits it. Otherwise it calls fd_gradient(). energy_and_gradient() first obtains the energy, then the gradient on the same calculation object, so analytic routes can share CC, HBar and left-state work. An optimizer’s forces are minus this gradient; pass gradients to CCpy’s adapter, which performs that sign conversion itself.

An analytic routine that raises an import, convergence or numerical error does not silently fall back to finite differences. has_analytic_gradient is a dispatch/capability check, not a substitute for testing an installed kernel and checking all electronic convergence flags. Most ordinary CC/EOM wrappers can retain unconverged iterates rather than raise exceptions; do not feed those values into an optimization or derivative comparison.

Which methods use analytic kernels?

Current methods-layer dispatch

Method

Analytic path

Additional conditions / interpretation

CCSD

Registered CCSD kernel.

RHF/UHF; no deleted virtuals or FNOs. Includes required Lambda/orbital response.

EOMCCSD

Registered EOMCCSD kernel.

Same reference restrictions; select the returned root; left/response work is required.

CRCC23

Variant A only.

Deterministic correction; other variants use numerical gradients.

CREOMCC23

Variant A only.

Gradient of the size-intensive delta-CR energy returned by energy(), not full_energy().

CCSDT_P

Conditional registration.

Requires the selected CC density module; check the actual object. No guarantee from class name alone.

Ground left-CC wrappers

Delegate to the parent’s analytic gradient when available.

Their energy is the right ground-state energy; they do not define a new surface.

MP2/MP3/MP4, ADC, STEOM, other CC/EOM levels and unregistered corrections

Finite-difference fallback.

Even when their energy equations use DF, they do not thereby have an analytic gradient.

ROHF, FNO references and ndelete != 0 do not currently pass the general methods-layer analytic eligibility check. Stochastic correction instances also use the fallback. Frozen occupied cores are distinct from deleted virtuals; a frozen core alone does not disable the registered RHF/UHF analytic routes.

For a deterministic CR-CC(2,3)_A example:

from ccpy import CRCC23

cr = CRCC23(cc, variant="A")
assert cr.has_analytic_gradient()
corrected_energy, corrected_gradient = cr.energy_and_gradient()

Use the same correction variant in energy and gradient. In particular, changing the default D correction to A changes the energy model, not just the derivative algorithm. CREOMCC23.full_energy() has no separate analytic gradient method in this wrapper; if that full energy is required, differentiate that scalar explicitly with a callback.

What an analytic gradient contains

CC energy depends on nuclear positions through AO integrals, basis overlap and molecular orbitals, as well as the correlated state. Analytic methods use left amplitudes/response equations to account for this dependence without displacing every coordinate. They include the appropriate integral and orbital response; contracting an unrelaxed stored RDM with an integral derivative alone generally does not produce the full molecular gradient.

cc.rdm1()/rdm2() expose derivative densities in the T1-transformed frame; Left states, densities, response and gradients explains that convention. These are not directly relaxed AO total-state RDMs. zvector_tol controls orbital-response solves on analytic routes that accept it. Use it only after checking that the requested route is analytic: arbitrary analytic-only kwargs are not filtered out when a method falls back to numerical differentiation and may reach energy() instead.

Force numerical gradients deliberately

numerical = cc.fd_gradient()  # bypasses analytic dispatch
analytic = cc.gradient()
import numpy as np
print("max difference (Eh/bohr):", np.max(np.abs(analytic - numerical)))
print("net gradient:", analytic.sum(axis=0))
xyz = np.array([xyz for _, xyz in mol.atoms])  # bohr
print("net torque diagnostic:", np.cross(xyz, analytic).sum(axis=0))

Set fd_step and fd_npoints on the method constructor. Do not call method.fd_gradient(step=...): its kwargs specify energy arguments such as root, not stencil settings. To change stencil settings after construction, recreate the method with explicit constructor options; this also preserves the settings correctly when at_geometry rebuilds the chain.

The available central stencils use 2, 4 or 6 points per coordinate, with leading truncation error of order h squared, h to the fourth or h to the sixth. For the four-point stencil:

\[\frac{\partial E}{\partial x}\simeq \frac{E(x-2h)-8E(x-h)+8E(x+h)-E(x+2h)}{12h}.\]

The default is h=1e-3 bohr and four points. A complete p-point gradient of an N-atom molecule requires p times 3N displaced scalar energy evaluations, in addition to any central energy requested separately. All displaced calculations rebuild SCF and the recorded correlated chain. This implementation does not propagate converged amplitudes/orbitals between geometries as a warm start.

Compare more than one displacement if precision matters. Making h smaller reduces truncation error but amplifies electronic energy noise approximately as energy_error/h. Tighten SCF, CC and left/response convergence before using very small steps. Translation/rotation sum rules are useful diagnostics at a fixed isolated-molecule geometry, but alone do not establish correctness of every gradient component or the electronic state identity.

Energy-only functions and partial checks

from ccpy import MP2
from ccpy.gradients.finite_difference import fd_gradient
from ccpy.optimize import fd_energy_and_gradient

def energy_at(molecule):
    method = MP2(RHF(molecule, verbose=0), nfrozen=1)
    energy = method.energy()
    if not method.converged:
        raise RuntimeError("MP2 setup did not converge")
    return energy

gradient = fd_gradient(energy_at, mol, step=1.e-3, npoints=4)
atom0_only = fd_gradient(energy_at, mol, atoms=[0], step=1.e-3, npoints=4)
callback = fd_energy_and_gradient(energy_at, step=1.e-3, npoints=4)
energy, gradient = callback(mol)

The function must return a scalar total energy for a supplied native Molecule and repeat the full electronic calculation. atoms contains zero-based atom indices; unchecked rows of the returned gradient are zero. Such a partial gradient is useful for validation, not an unconstrained geometry optimization. The helper fd_energy_and_gradient packages numerical differentiation for the pysisyphus interface.

Excited-state gradients and state following

from ccpy import EOMCCSD

eom = EOMCCSD(cc)
eom.initial_guess(method="cisd", multiplicity=1, nacto=2, nactu=2,
                  roots_per_irrep={"A": 4}, roots=[1, 3])
total, gradient = eom.energy_and_gradient(root=1)
numerical = eom.fd_gradient(root=1)

Here root=1 is the second returned state, seeded by guess index 3. It is not the second guess in the original pool. Calling energy_and_gradient() without root on a multiroot method does not define a single scalar surface: energy() returns an array while the analytic gradient route defaults to root 0; numerical differentiation can reject a nonscalar energy. This is not the scalar energy/gradient pair required by the optimization calculator. Always select one returned root in an explicit callback for excited-state optimization.

Keep the same physical state at displaced geometries and along an optimization or IRC. at_geometry copies guess settings; it does not provide a universal cross-geometry state-overlap tracker. Inspect character, irreps and neighboring states near avoided crossings and degeneracies. The TS Hessian mode index is unrelated to an electronic EOM root index. See Geometry optimization, transition states and IRC validation for a scalar excited-state callback and optimizer setup.

FNOs, discrete spaces and smooth surfaces

Numerical differentiation evaluates the completed energy() by default. For FNOs it rebuilds the density, virtual selection, transformed factors and low-level completion at each displacement. Analytic gradients do not currently include these FNO-selection derivatives. A cutoff crossing, changing protected span or lost target root can make the energy nonsmooth. Check retained counts, root identity and step/threshold convergence before trusting gradients or Hessian signs. A fixed retained count alone does not establish smoothness at degeneracies.

Selected P/Q spaces and external CI data must also remain meaningful across geometries. AdaptiveCCPQ.at_geometry reuses its recorded determinant spaces; that is different from rerunning independent adaptive selection at each geometry. Ordinary user-supplied determinant labels are not automatic orbital tracking. For stochastic energies, differentiation can amplify sampling noise; use deterministic corrections for derivative checks and stationary-point work unless a consistent noise-controlled protocol has been established.

Runnable validation

OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python examples/methods_api/geometry_workflows.py \
    --task gradient-check --method ccsd --basis sto-3g --out-dir gradient-check

This example compares automatic analytic CCSD gradients with an independently displaced energy gradient at a distorted water geometry. Method-layer tests are in tests/methods/test_method_gradients.py; additional SCF and correction checks are in tests/scf. The example’s small basis demonstrates the workflow, not a benchmark-quality geometry or barrier.
