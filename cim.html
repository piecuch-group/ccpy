Cluster-in-molecule local correlation

Important

The CIM wrappers are experimental and are not fully tested. Limited canonical-limit, partition, subsystem and multilevel checks exist, but do not establish broad accuracy, robustness, scaling or geometry-derivative validation. Benchmark the chosen model and thresholds against a canonical calculation on representative small systems before scientific use.

Every CIM calculation issues a CIMDevelopmentWarning (exported from ccpy) to say so; filter it with warnings.simplefilter("ignore", CIMDevelopmentWarning) once the model has been checked for your systems.

Cluster-in-molecule (CIM) splits a ground-state correlated calculation into independent calculations for small orbital subsystems and assembles the correlation energy from the contributions of the occupied localized orbitals (LMOs),

\[\Delta E = \sum_{i'} \delta E_{i'}, \qquad \delta E_{i'} = \frac{1}{M_{i'}} \sum_{\{P_{i'}\}} \delta E_{i'}(\{P_{i'}\}),\]

where \(\{P_{i'}\}\) runs over the \(M_{i'}\) subsystems in which the LMO \(i'\) is central. CIM is enabled on any supported method with cim=, in the same way as FNOs:

from ccpy import Molecule, RHF, CCSD, CRCC23, MP2, CIMOptions

mf = RHF(Molecule(geometry, "6-31g*", auxiliary_basis="def2-universal-jkfit"))
cim = CRCC23(mf, nfrozen=ncore, cim=CIMOptions(zeta=0.003))
energy = cim.energy()
cim.domains          # the subsystems (CIMDomains)
cim.subsystems       # orbital counts, method, and energy of each subsystem

The constructor returns a ccpy.methods.cim.CIM object; method options (solver settings, variant of CR-CC(2,3), …) are passed on to the subsystem calculations.

The implemented class filter accepts ground-state CCBase subclasses, MP2, and the concrete CRCC23 and AdaptiveCCPQ classes. Ordinary CCSD/CCSDT/CC3-style energies are assembled from their T1/T2 energy expression. Acceptance by that filter is not proof that every subclass or parameterized input is usable: selected CC(P) lists and external CI data need a subsystem-basis adaptation that this wrapper does not generally provide. Begin with MP2, CCSD, CCSDT, CRCC23 or AdaptiveCCPQ, which have focused canonical-limit tests.

Adaptive CC(P;Q) runs its macroiterations unchanged in every subsystem: each subsystem grows its own P space of triples (in its QCMO basis) by its own moment contributions and stops by its own energy test (energy_tolerance applies to the subsystem energy). The central energy is the CC(P) energy from T1/T2 plus the central part of the CC(P;3)_D correction (two-body approximation) on the P space of the last macroiteration, partitioned like CR-CC(2,3) with the triples sum skipping the P space. With percentage=[..., 100.0] every subsystem reaches CCSDT, so the calculation converges to CIM-CCSDT. Its limitations:

  • the triples are ranked by their contributions to the whole subsystem correction, not to its central part, so some are spent on environment-only excitations;

  • the P spaces are labeled by subsystem QCMOs, which change with the geometry, so at_geometry (fixed domains, finite differences) reselects them rather than reusing them;

  • semi-stochastic corrections (stochastic=) are not supported.

from ccpy.methods.corrections import AdaptiveCCPQ

local = AdaptiveCCPQ(mf, nfrozen=ncore, percentage=[0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0],
                     cim=CIMOptions(zeta=0.003))
energy = local.energy() The reference must be closed-shell

RHF without point-group symmetry or deleted virtuals.

Out-of-the-box setup and what is returned

from ccpy import CIM, CIMOptions, CCSD, MP2, Molecule, RHF

mol = Molecule("O 0 0 0; H 0 0 .96; H .93 0 -.24", "6-31g",
               auxiliary_basis="def2-universal-jkfit", symmetry=False)
mf = RHF(mol, verbose=0)
local = CCSD(mf, nfrozen=1, cim=CIMOptions(zeta=0.003))
assert isinstance(local, CIM)  # this is a CIM wrapper, not a CCSD amplitude object
total = local.energy()
print(local.reference_energy, local.correlation_energy, total)
print(local.domains.subsystems)
for subsystem in local.subsystems:
    print(subsystem)

cim= is intercepted by MethodBase.__new__. You can also construct CIM(CCSD, mf, nfrozen=1, cim=CIMOptions(...)) explicitly. Start from native SCF or a Reference, not a previously solved CC/left/correction object; CIM creates its own subsystem calculations. Omit cim or pass None for a canonical calculation; cim=False is not the disabling convention here.

The total is the full-system determinant energy plus assembled central-LMO correlation contributions. Each subsystems entry contains method, ncentral, noccupied, nunoccupied, natoms and energy. That energy is the weighted central contribution, not a complete subsystem total that should have its own SCF energy added again. raw_energy() equals energy(): no automatic full-minus-truncated composite correction is attached.

local.domains is a CIMDomains object containing subsystems and a (nLMO,natoms) array of atomic populations. Each underlying CIMSubsystem has central, occupied, atoms and method. Orbital and atom indices are zero based; central LMOs are listed first in occupied. The wrapper exposes summarized subsystem results, not a global canonical T/L/R operator. It cannot serve as a normal CC parent for EOM or left-state composition.

Use a canonical-limit check before a threshold sweep:

canonical = CCSD(mf, nfrozen=1).energy()
limit = CCSD(mf, nfrozen=1, cim=CIMOptions(zeta=0.)).energy()
print("Canonical-limit error:", limit - canonical)
for zeta in (0.01, 0.003, 0.001):
    approximation = CCSD(mf, nfrozen=1, cim=CIMOptions(zeta=zeta))
    print(zeta, approximation.energy(), approximation.subsystems)

Smaller zeta generally expands occupied environments, but domain merging can change discretely; monotonic energy errors and universal accuracy at the paper’s thresholds are not guaranteed. On a small molecule every selected domain can already span the full system, so a fast small example is not a local-correlation scaling benchmark.

Method

The implementation follows W. Li, P. Piecuch, J. R. Gour, and S. Li, J. Chem. Phys. 131, 114109 (2009), with the single-environment subsystems of W. Li and P. Piecuch, J. Phys. Chem. A 114, 8644 (2010), and the multilevel scheme of J. Phys. Chem. A 114, 6721 (2010).

  1. The correlated occupied orbitals are Boys localized.

  2. Subsystems (SECIM). Every non-hydrogen atom, together with the hydrogen atoms bonded to it, collects as central orbitals the LMOs whose Mulliken charge (of two electrons) on one of these atoms exceeds central_charge (0.3). The environment are the LMOs \(j\) with \(|\langle i|f|j\rangle| > \zeta\) for a central \(i\). A subsystem whose occupied LMOs are contained in another one is merged into it (its central LMOs become central there). A single threshold \(\zeta\) controls the calculation: \(\zeta = 0\) gives one subsystem and the canonical result. Since subsystems are anchored on atoms, they do not rearrange unnecessarily as the nuclei move.

  3. Local subsystem orbitals. The AO domain of an occupied LMO (core included) holds its largest-charge atoms whose charges add up to domain_charge (1.98); the AO domain of a subsystem is the union over its LMOs. Its extended domain adds every atom whose anchor (itself, or the non-hydrogen atom a hydrogen is bonded to) lies within buffer_radius (4 angstrom) of the AO domain. The occupied LMOs whose AO domains lie in the extended domain are projected onto its AOs (least squares) and symmetrically orthonormalized; the subsystem’s own ones are its occupied orbitals. Its unoccupied orbitals span the exact orthogonal complement of those projected LMOs in the extended domain (the span of its PAOs), restricted to the orbitals that differ from their projection onto the AO-domain atoms by less than eta (0.5) in norm. Every subsystem orbital is a combination of the AOs of its extended domain only, and the subsystem orbitals are exactly orthonormal.

  4. Quasi-canonical orbitals. The occupied and unoccupied orbitals of each subsystem are rotated to the QCMOs that diagonalize its Fock blocks. Each subsystem is an ordinary Reference holding only its own orbitals: its Fock matrix is that of the full system (the other occupied LMOs enter through it), and its two-electron integrals are density fitted with the auxiliary functions of the extended domain, formed one auxiliary atom at a time. No full-system MO integrals are built, and the unmodified method runs on the subsystem. The occupied and unoccupied subsystem orbitals are not exactly the HF ones, so the subsystem Fock matrix has small occupied-unoccupied elements; CC methods include them, MP2 neglects them.

  5. Mixed-index energies. With \(R\) the rotation from the LMOs to the QCMOs, \(\delta E_{i'}(\{P\})\) is the energy expression with its first occupied index rotated back to the LMO \(i'\) (Eqs. (15)-(22) of the 2009 paper). For the CC energy, \(\sum_a f_{i'a} t^a_{i'} + \tfrac12 \sum_{j,a<b} v_{i'j}^{ab} \tau^{ab}_{i'j}\). For the triples correction of CR-CC(2,3), \(\tfrac13 \sum \ell^{i'jk}_{abc} \mathfrak{M}_{i'jk}^{abc}\): the Fortran kernels are called with the moment inputs (HBar blocks and \(T_2\)) replaced by their derivatives under the occupied transformation \(e^{\lambda P}\), which yields \((P_1 + P_2 + P_3)\mathfrak{M}\) exactly, with \(P\) the central projector weighted by \(1/M_{i'}\). In the QCMO basis the triples keep their canonical (diagonal-denominator) form, so the corrections remain noniterative.

Potential energy surfaces

By default the subsystems are determined at every geometry (they adapt to the nuclear arrangement through their atom anchors). Where a reaction coordinate changes them (e.g. two subsystems merge as a bond breaks), the energy can jump. The fixed-domain variant reuses the subsystems of one geometry everywhere:

ref = CRCC23(mf_re, nfrozen=ncore, cim=CIMOptions(zeta=0.01))
ref.energy()
for mf in scan:
    e = CRCC23(mf, nfrozen=ncore, cim=CIMOptions(zeta=0.01, domains=ref.domains)).energy()

The LMOs of the new geometry are matched to those of the reference geometry by their atomic Mulliken populations (an assignment problem), and each subsystem keeps its AO-domain atoms. at_geometry (and so finite-difference gradients, which are the only gradients CIM provides) always use the fixed domains. For a gradient or an optimization, solve the reference CIM calculation first so its domains exist:

from ccpy.optimize import optimize_geometry

local.energy()
gradient = local.gradient()  # numerical energy differences, no analytic CIM response
# Potentially expensive; this remains an experimental numerical surface.
optimized = optimize_geometry(local, thresh="gau_tight")

Frozen domain membership does not freeze the LMOs or QCMO coefficients. The orbitals are relocalized/rematched and subsystem orbitals rebuilt at each geometry. Population-based assignments can become ambiguous at degeneracies, and changes in the number of unoccupied orbitals passing eta can still affect smoothness. Verify energy/gradient continuity before using CIM for minima, transition states, frequencies or IRCs. Static property convenience methods currently request an analytic property gradient and therefore are unavailable on CIM; a consistent fixed-domain finite-field scalar-energy callback is the generic alternative described in Multipole moments and harmonic IR intensities.

Constructing a fresh CIM object independently at every displaced geometry without domains=... is a different, adaptively selected surface. It need not give the same derivative as local.fd_gradient() or local.at_geometry.

Multilevel CIM

levels assigns higher-level methods to atoms; the method CIM is called on treats the rest:

cim = MP2(mf, nfrozen=ncore, cim=CIMOptions(levels={CRCC23: [0, 1]}))

A subsystem is treated by the highest level for which one of its central LMOs lies entirely on that level’s atoms (hydrogen atoms follow the atom they are bonded to). A subsystem is not merged into one of a lower level, and an LMO central in subsystems of different levels contributes their average. The keys are method classes, not method instances; atom lists use zero-based full-molecule indices. Dictionary insertion order defines the ranking: list the highest level first. Hydrogens follow their heavy-atom anchors. The assignment is based on an LMO’s atomic population set, not simply a per-atom additive correlation energy.

mixed = MP2(mf, nfrozen=1, cim=CIMOptions(
    zeta=0.003, levels={CCSD: [0]},
))
mixed_total = mixed.energy()
print([row["method"] for row in mixed.subsystems])

This upgrades qualifying subsystems anchored on atom 0 to CCSD and leaves the others at MP2. A single water may all qualify, so test a separated-fragment model when demonstrating mixed levels. Shared solver options reach subsystem methods, but method-specific kwargs such as variant are forwarded only when the subsystem’s class is the wrapper’s base method. levels does not accept per-level constructor option dictionaries; an upgraded CRCC23 class therefore uses its own default variant.

Options

zeta (0.003)

environment threshold; SECIM(0.003) and SECIM(0.001) are the settings of the 2010 paper.

central_charge (0.3), domain_charge (1.98), bond_tolerance (0.168 angstrom)

LMO-to-atom assignments; X-H bonds use covalent radii.

buffer_radius (4.0 angstrom)

buffer atoms of the extended domains, as in the 2009 paper. Much smaller values let occupied orbitals of excluded neighbors leak into the unoccupied space; this is detected (an unoccupied QCMO below the occupied ones) and raises a ValueError.

eta (0.5)

selection of the unoccupied orbitals of a subsystem (step 12 of the 2009 paper, which recommends 0.5 when the unoccupied orbitals are strictly orthonormalized).

levels, domains

multilevel methods and fixed subsystems.

localization_tol (1e-12), localization_maxiter (1000)

Boys localization convergence and iteration limit.

verbose (False)

print the output of the subsystem calculations.

Differences from the papers

  • The subsystem orbitals are kept in the extended domain (AO domain plus buffer) rather than projected back onto the AO domain, and they are strictly orthonormal (an option in the 2009 paper). The eta selection is applied to the whole unoccupied space of the extended domain at once (the subspace with weight above 1 - eta^2 on the AO-domain atoms) instead of to its Boys-localized orbitals. With an extended domain covering the molecule and one subsystem, the canonical calculation is recovered.

  • The two-electron integrals are fitted locally, with the auxiliary functions of the extended domain.

  • X-H bonds use covalent radii plus bond_tolerance.

  • Among the noniterative corrections only CR-CC(2,3) and the CC(P;3) correction of adaptive CC(P;Q) have the triples partition; CCSD(T), CC(t;3), CC(P;3) with a given P space, EOM, and open-shell references are not supported yet. Adaptive CC(P;Q) is not part of the papers.

Restrictions, custom domains and validation status

CIM is currently closed-shell native RHF, C1, without deleted virtuals, with density fitting (cholesky_tei molecules are not supported). It does not support UHF/ROHF, EE/IP/EA/DEA/DIP/SF EOM, ADC or STEOM. FNO+CIM composition is unsupported, including starting from an already FNO-reduced reference. Noniterative CCSD(T), CC(t;3), standalone CC(P;3) and quadruples corrections do not have this wrapper’s correction partition. Stochastic CIM correction sampling is not implemented: the CRCC23 and adaptive CC(P;Q) central correction paths directly call deterministic kernels. Do not infer availability from kwargs that a subsystem class otherwise accepts.

For advanced fixed/custom domains, CIMDomains is exported from CCpy; CIMSubsystem is available from ccpy.methods.cim. Custom central labels, occupied lists, atom domains and population references must describe the same correlated LMO space and molecule. Every occupied LMO must be covered as central with consistent multiplicity; central orbitals must occupy the leading positions of each subsystem list. The wrapper is not a general validator/remapper for arbitrary selected determinants or external orbital labels.

The current result keeps only summarized subsystem counts/energies. Inspect subsystem solver output with CIMOptions(verbose=True) and use tight solver settings: the wrapper’s final converged flag alone is not an independent check that every inner iterative solve converged.

tests/methods/test_cim.py contains focused checks for full-space limits (MP2, CCSD, CCSDT, CRCC23), a dense mixed-index CRCC23_A partition oracle, water-trimer subsystem construction, truncated extended domains (n-decane), the buffer-too-small check, fixed-domain matching, multilevel assignment and rejected unsupported classes/references. These are limited implementation checks, not a broad method/reference/basis benchmark or a production scaling test. Derivatives, large/localized systems and general reaction paths remain insufficiently validated.
